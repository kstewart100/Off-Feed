# Blurb examples

Each pair shows a blurb from Issue No. 1 next to a version that breaks the spec.

## Stewart Hicks — The Problem With Colorful Houses
**Good:** An architecture professor traces how American houses went beige, gray and black, starting with San Francisco's Painted Ladies and running through lead paint and public-health history.
**Bad:** You'll never look at your neighborhood the same way after this fascinating deep dive!
**Why:** The good version names the subject and the path the video takes, both from its description. The bad one is hype, tells the viewer what to feel, and says nothing about the content.

## Linus Boman — The secret history of font piracy
**Good:** A designer and type historian looks at how fonts get copied and resold. One example is the famous anti-DVD-piracy PSA, which itself used a pirated font.
**Bad:** Linus exposes the shocking scandal that the type industry doesn't want you to know about.
**Why:** "Scandal" and "doesn't want you to know" are claims the description doesn't support. The specific example (the PSA font) is more interesting than any adjective.

## Laura Kampf — Is It Okay To Steal Ideas?
**Good:** A designer-maker on copying, influence and credit in maker work.
**Bad:** Laura builds an amazing project and shares her favorite new 5-axis CNC machine.
**Why:** This video's description opens with a sponsor (a CNC Kickstarter). A blurb drawn from the first lines of the description would describe the ad, not the video. When the description is mostly sponsorship, write from the title and keep it short.

## Adam Neely — Sungazer Plus Live at the Blue Note
**Good:** A full big-band concert film recorded July 1–2, 2025, at the Blue Note in New York.
**Bad:** A 2026 concert from Adam Neely's band.
**Why:** The upload date (June 2026) isn't the performance date (July 2025). Dates in a blurb have to say what they refer to.
